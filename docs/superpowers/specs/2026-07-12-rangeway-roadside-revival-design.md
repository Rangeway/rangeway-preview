# Rangeway Roadside Revival Redesign

Date: July 12, 2026

Status: Approved design direction, pending user review of this written specification

## 1. Objective

Redesign the complete Rangeway marketing site so it feels warm, lively, distinctive, and representative of the product. The site should communicate hospitality before infrastructure and make the promise of a Rangeway stop emotionally legible before explaining the network.

The approved creative direction is **Roadside Revival**: a modern return to optimistic American roadside hospitality, expressed with mid-century geometry, tactile warmth, bold wayfinding, disciplined typography, and a precise technical edge.

The redesign must stop feeling like a restrained corporate folio. It must also avoid becoming a luxury hotel site, a dark technology site, or a nostalgic theme park.

## 2. Approved Design Principles

1. **Experience first.** The first story is how the stop feels for a driver. The network formats follow after the hospitality promise is understood.
2. **Hospitality and warmth dominate.** Technology appears through precision, reliability, labels, and interaction rather than cold imagery or technical jargon.
3. **Playful but disciplined.** Bold color, geometric composition, and roadside cues create energy. Typography, spacing, and content restraint keep the result credible.
4. **Retro without kitsch.** Use one strong roadside reference at a time. Avoid novelty stickers, excessive badges, starbursts, faux-vintage textures, and decorative clutter.
5. **Warm without luxury signaling.** The brand should feel accessible, human, and inviting. Avoid resort minimalism, muted luxury palettes, or precious editorial styling.
6. **Vision through imagery.** Large renders and hospitality details sell the future. Informational modules organize the story but do not carry it alone.
7. **Each page earns its design.** Shared rules create consistency, but every page receives an individual design review and can depart from its family template when its content requires it.

## 3. Content Contract

The redesign preserves the current site's substantive content, approved claims, fixed terminology, and strategic boundaries. Content may be reorganized and lightly edited to improve rhythm, hierarchy, clarity, and page pacing.

The redesign does not treat the content as a blank slate. It also does not force existing copy into the current section order.

Existing Rangeway writing rules remain in force:

- No em dashes or double hyphens in user-facing copy.
- No sentences beginning with "And."
- No fragments, hashtags, or unsupported statistics.
- Use Waystation, Basecamp, Summit, Trailkeepers, Lookouts, drivers, and hotel operators exactly as defined.
- Keep driver's lounge lowercase.
- Do not call formats stations, units, rooms, or cabins.
- Property and host acquisition remains outside the public Rangeway brand funnel.

## 4. Global Navigation

The redesign preserves the current primary navigation architecture and page set:

1. The Network
2. Our Story
3. Team
4. Partners
5. Investors
6. Newsroom
7. Contact

Desktop navigation remains directly visible when space permits. Mobile uses a dedicated menu treatment. The visual design may change, but route names and destinations stay intact unless the user separately approves a navigation change.

## 5. Homepage Architecture

The approved homepage is an **image-led Roadside Field Guide**.

### 5.1 Hero

- Lead with a bold, warm statement about a better roadside stop.
- Pair oversized Raleway display type with one strong aspirational image.
- Use Rangeway Sun as the optimistic surface and Highway Navy for navigation and structure.
- Use a clear primary action that moves into the experience or network.
- Do not use a sticker, seal, or floating badge on the hero.

### 5.2 Arrival Rhythm

Immediately after the hero, present a short three-part wayfinding rhythm:

1. Arrive
2. Recharge
3. Continue

This communicates the driver journey before introducing features or formats.

### 5.3 The Experience

Lead the body of the page with the felt experience of Rangeway. Use a large image collage or sequence showing interiors, place, warm light, and the transition away from road fatigue.

This section should answer: "Why would I want to stop here?"

### 5.4 The Field Guide

Translate the hospitality promise into a concise set of experience principles. The approved conceptual set is:

- Good light
- Real comfort
- Room to settle
- Easy momentum

Each principle must be paired with imagery. This must not become a generic four-icon feature grid.

### 5.5 The Network

Introduce Waystation, Basecamp, and Summit only after the experience is understood. Present all three as different expressions of one hospitality standard, using image-led format cards and concise descriptions.

### 5.6 The Vision

Use a cinematic image and concise vision statement to connect the immediate driver experience to the larger roadside network Rangeway is building.

The public project order is:

1. Bozeman
2. Mojave
3. St. Louis

Status language and outbound links continue to come from the current verified content and configuration.

### 5.7 Closing Action

End with a bold, simple invitation to explore the network or continue into the most relevant next page. The closing should feel energetic and optimistic, not corporate.

## 6. Visual System

### 6.1 Core Palette

The proposed Roadside Revival palette is:

- **Highway Navy** `#17313E`: navigation, dark bands, technical structure, and high-contrast controls.
- **Rangeway Sun** `#F2B64C`: optimistic primary surface, wayfinding emphasis, and warm energy.
- **Oxide Red** `#993A28`: primary actions and selective rustic contrast.
- **Roadmap Cream** `#F7E6C4`: calm reading surfaces and warm negative space.

Before implementation, every foreground and background pairing must be checked against WCAG 2.1 AA. If an exact color requires adjustment for contrast, preserve its perceptual role and document the final token value.

### 6.2 Typography

The approved two-font system remains:

- **Raleway** is the brand voice. Use it for display headlines, section headings, navigation, buttons, labels, captions, and wayfinding.
- **Source Sans 3** is the reading voice. Use it for paragraphs, longer descriptions, forms, legal content, and other sustained reading.

Raleway should be visually dominant across the site to maintain continuity with the logo and wordmark. Source Sans 3 prevents longer pages from becoming rigid or stuffy.

Display typography should be large, heavy, compact, and energetic. Body typography should remain open, warm, and comfortable to read.

### 6.3 Shape Language

- Prefer square and lightly rounded cards over pervasive pills.
- Use arcs, ellipses, and asymmetric crops selectively to reference mid-century roadside architecture.
- Keep primary buttons compact and lightly squared rather than fully pill-shaped.
- Use rules, route strips, and destination labels as structural wayfinding devices.
- Avoid decorative stickers, novelty seals, and gratuitous badges.

### 6.4 Imagery

Imagery is provisionally placed during structural design. Final asset selection, crop, sequencing, and responsive focal points happen during the page-by-page iteration phase.

The image strategy should prioritize:

- Warm interiors and visible hospitality cues
- Dusk and golden-hour exterior light
- Clear architectural character
- Real locations and credible future renders
- Scenes that make the stop feel worth planning around

Avoid imagery that centers equipment without people-oriented context, repeats the same render too often, or makes Rangeway feel like a conventional charging operator.

### 6.5 Motion

Motion should add momentum and technical polish:

- Short directional reveals
- Clean image pushes on hover
- Arrow travel and color inversion for actions
- Optional route-line progression where it improves orientation

Avoid constant animation, floating decorative objects, ornamental bounce, and heavy parallax. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and ensure all content is visible and usable without animation or JavaScript.

## 7. Interior Page Families

The existing page set is organized into three design families. These are starting frameworks, not rigid templates.

### 7.1 Explore Pages

Pages:

- The Network
- Waystation
- Basecamp
- Summit

Purpose:

- Help drivers understand the formats
- Make the experience tangible
- Explain the operating standard without leading with specifications

Design behavior:

- Image-forward hero and galleries
- Shared format navigation
- Hospitality experience before amenities
- Clear comparison and cross-navigation between formats
- Technical details presented as supporting proof

### 7.2 Story Pages

Pages:

- Our Story
- Team
- Partners
- Commitments

Purpose:

- Establish the human and operational credibility behind the brand
- Show how hospitality experience, EV community, and specialist partners come together

Design behavior:

- Portraits, timelines, partner proof, and place-based imagery
- More editorial breathing room than Explore pages
- Warm, direct narrative rather than corporate biography treatment
- Page-specific visual QA for portraits, logos, and content density

### 7.3 Action Pages

Pages:

- Investors
- Contact
- Contact Thanks

Purpose:

- Make the next step clear
- Present credible project and operating context
- Support conversion without resembling a SaaS funnel

Design behavior:

- Strong hierarchy and concise proof
- Verified project order and status
- Clear contact or investor action
- Simple, accessible forms with visible recovery states

Legal and utility pages retain the shared typography, palette, navigation, and footer but prioritize reading comfort over expressive composition.

## 8. Component and Code Architecture

Implementation should preserve the existing Astro application and create focused reusable components rather than one page-specific global stylesheet.

Expected component boundaries include:

- Global navigation and mobile menu
- Footer
- Hero variants by page family
- Arrival rhythm strip
- Experience collage
- Field Guide principle cards
- Format cards and shared format navigation
- Project status rows
- Image figure and caption treatment
- Page-family section headers
- Buttons and text links
- Contact form and feedback states

Content shared across pages should remain data-driven where practical. Format names, routes, project order, statuses, external links, and contact destinations should have one authoritative source so the homepage and interior pages cannot silently diverge.

## 9. Responsive Behavior

Responsive design is part of the composition, not a later collapse of desktop columns.

- Navigation switches to a deliberate mobile menu.
- Image collages become intentional vertical sequences.
- Multi-column Field Guide cards become a paced reading order.
- Format cards preserve image impact and clear labels.
- Large headlines reduce without losing their compact character.
- Elliptical image crops may become full-width rectangles when the mobile crop would hide the subject.
- Project order remains Bozeman, Mojave, St. Louis at every breakpoint.
- No page may introduce horizontal scrolling.

## 10. Accessibility and Resilience

- WCAG 2.1 AA is the minimum contrast and interaction standard.
- Maintain visible focus states and complete keyboard operation.
- The mobile menu must contain focus while open and restore focus when closed.
- All meaningful images require hospitality-aware alternative text.
- Decorative images use empty alternative text.
- Content must remain visible if JavaScript fails.
- Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Contact forms require clear validation, submission, success, timeout, and retry states.
- External links must retain appropriate security attributes.
- Preview indexing controls, canonical URLs, sitemap behavior, and form return URLs remain centralized and environment-aware.

## 11. Validation Strategy

Implementation is complete only after:

1. Existing automated regression tests pass.
2. Astro type and content checks pass.
3. Production and preview builds pass.
4. Every primary route is visually reviewed at desktop and mobile widths.
5. Navigation, menu, keyboard flow, focus states, and reduced motion are verified.
6. Light and dark behavior is either intentionally supported or intentionally removed as part of the redesign. It must not remain as an accidental partial theme.
7. Contact validation and recovery states are tested without sending an unintended real message.
8. Image sizing, loading, intrinsic dimensions, responsive sources, and focal crops are checked page by page.
9. No horizontal overflow is present.
10. The user approves each page's visual result before the redesign is considered finished.

## 12. Page-by-Page Review Contract

The homepage architecture, visual system, typography, and interior-page families are approved as the shared direction.

Individual pages are not pre-approved by this specification. During implementation, each page receives its own visual review. The user may provide page-specific notes on composition, imagery, copy pacing, alignment, portraits, logos, forms, and responsive behavior. Those notes override family defaults for that page.

Final image choice is deliberately deferred to this page-by-page phase.

## 13. Non-Goals

- Rebranding Rangeway or changing the logo and wordmark
- Changing the current primary navigation page set
- Creating a host-acquisition funnel for Rangeway
- Adding unsupported claims, statistics, destinations, partners, or links
- Reintroducing the rejected Folio layout under new colors
- Recreating a literal vintage roadside or Googie theme
- Deploying to production before local visual approval

## 14. Approved Decisions Summary

- Creative direction: Roadside Revival
- Personality: balanced playfulness
- Primary emotion: hospitality and warmth
- Narrative order: experience before network
- Homepage structure: image-led Roadside Field Guide
- Navigation: preserve the current page set
- Hero: no sticker or seal
- Project order: Bozeman, Mojave, St. Louis
- Typography: Raleway plus Source Sans 3
- Images: provisional until page-by-page review
- Interior pages: three flexible families with individual iteration
